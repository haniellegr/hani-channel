# Roteiro 03 — Why I Can't Go to Bed (Even When I'm Tired)

> **Primeiro roteiro do pivô wellness (ver `docs/07`).** Versão 2: escrito a partir da
> entrevista da Hani (resumo abaixo). Tudo o que é pessoal no texto veio das respostas
> dela; nada foi inventado.

- **Pilar:** sono e ritmo · **Tipo:** evergreen
- **Fenômeno (rótulo na tela):** **Bedtime Procrastination** (termo científico,
  Kroese et al., 2014) → **Revenge Bedtime Procrastination** (versão popular) + a virada
  **The Second Shift** (dois termos já existentes; dar crédito, não inventar nomes)
- **Duração-alvo:** 7–9 min (~950 palavras faladas; dá pra crescer com pausas e B-roll)
- **Produção:** Hani falando + B-roll próprio gravado à noite (celular no escuro,

  do parque. Tipografia nos rótulos.

## Entrevista (resumo — a fonte do roteiro)

1. Dorme meia-noite ou mais; no fim de semana ~2h, vendo série depois de um dia de
   faxina, compras e comida da semana. Está tentando mudar.
2. Depois do trabalho fica no celular e adia o banho; tomar coragem pro banho,
   skincare, louça, marmitas e tudo que fica pra noite é difícil.
3. Sente arrependimento e já sabe que vai acordar cansada e não vai render.
4. Sempre dormiu tarde: estuda à noite desde os 12; na faculdade chegava depois das
   23h e fazia trabalho de madrugada. Hoje tem 35.
5. Cansaço o dia todo, rende menos no trabalho, sono, e atrapalha o emagrecimento.
6. Sim, a noite é o único tempo pra lazer, fazer o que gosta, relaxar.
7. Usa CBD às vezes; já manteve por um tempo a regra "sem tela e sem luz forte depois
   das 21h", com velas, banho e preparo pra dormir. O difícil é manter.
8. Dormiu bem pela última vez nas férias: perto de um parque, com a família, celular
   de lado, e a mãe e a irmã cuidaram da cozinha — alívio das "300 tarefas" diárias.
9. Mesmo cansada, se esforça pra continuar acordada, fazendo coisas ou no celular:
   "meu cérebro não quer desligar à noite".

**Insight central (saiu da resposta 8):** o sono voltou quando alguém tirou as tarefas
dela. O problema não é a noite, é o dia — e o trabalho que começa depois do trabalho.

## Descrição (rascunho)
I've been going to bed too late since I was twelve. I always blamed my discipline.
But the one week I slept well in years taught me something different: it was never
really about the night. It was about everything the day leaves for it.
(fontes e créditos de B-roll no fim)

## Fontes a checar antes de gravar (colocar na descrição)
- Kroese et al. (2014), Utrecht: artigo que definiu *bedtime procrastination*.
- Origem de *revenge bedtime procrastination*: expressão popularizada nas redes
  chinesas (~2018–2020) por quem trabalhava jornadas longas.
- Arlie Hochschild, *The Second Shift* (1989): o trabalho doméstico depois do trabalho.
- Recomendação de 7+ horas por noite para adultos (CDC / American Academy of Sleep Medicine).
- Sono curto x hormônios do apetite (grelina/leptina) e peso: usar revisão científica.
- Cronotipo ("night owl") tem componente biológico: não afirmar que é só hábito.
- Estresse e estado de alerta à noite (*hyperarousal*, cortisol que não cai): usar
  fonte confiável. "Tired but wired" é expressão popular, não termo científico.

# SCRIPT

### 1 · Gancho pessoal (0–30s)

`[BR: TV acesa no escuro, relógio do celular marcando 2:07]`

It's Saturday. It's past two in the morning. And I'm watching one more episode of a show I'm not even paying attention to anymore.

`[TC — close, calm]`

I spent the whole day cleaning the house, buying groceries, cooking for the week. And now that it's finally over, I just... won't let it end. Even though I know exactly how I'm going to feel tomorrow.

I've been doing this for as long as I can remember. So today I want to figure out why.

### 2 · Nomear o padrão

`[TC]`

It turns out this has a name, and it's an actual scientific term. In 2014, a group of researchers in the Netherlands called it *bedtime procrastination*: going to bed later than you planned, when nothing is actually stopping you.

`[TEXT: BEDTIME PROCRASTINATION — Kroese et al., 2014]`

So yes. Procrastination. The same thing we do with the work we don't want to start, we do with sleep.

`[TEXT: REVENGE BEDTIME PROCRASTINATION]`

And at some point, people online added one word to it: *revenge*. Because that's exactly what it feels like. Like you're taking back a few hours from a day that took everything from you.

### 3 · Desnormalizar

`[TC]`

For years, my explanation was simple: I'm just a night person. I have no discipline. I need to put the phone down.

But here's the thing. I started studying at night when I was twelve. In college, I'd get home after eleven and do my assignments in the middle of the night. I'm thirty-five now.

`[TC — slower]`

So I have to ask myself: was I born a night owl? Or was I trained to be one? Maybe a bit of both. Some people really are wired for late nights. But I don't think my body chose this alone. I think my life has been choosing it for me for more than twenty years.

### 4 · Por que acontece

`[TC]`

When I look at my evenings honestly, I can see four things going on.

**One: the night is the only time that feels like mine.**

`[BR: chegando em casa, bolsa no sofá, celular na mão]`

I get home from work tired, and before anything else, I pick up my phone. Just for a minute. And that minute is the only part of the day nobody is asking anything from me. So of course I hold on to it.

**Two: the day doesn't end when work ends.**

`[BR: louça na pia, potes de marmita, skincare na pia do banheiro]`

Because after the phone, there's the shower. And skincare. And the dishes. And the lunches for tomorrow. And all the other little things that somehow always end up at night. And I have to find the courage to start every single one of them.

`[TEXT: THE SECOND SHIFT]`

There's a name for this too. Back in 1989, the sociologist Arlie Hochschild called it *the second shift*: all the work that starts when your actual work is over. She was writing about working women, and I think a lot of us will recognize ourselves in that.

**Three: the way I rest is designed to keep me awake.**

`[BR: dedo rolando o feed; "next episode in 5 seconds"]`

A book has a last page. A feed doesn't. And streaming starts the next episode before I've even decided if I want it. On top of that, the light from screens tells my brain it's still daytime. `[checar fonte]`

**Four: my brain doesn't want to switch off.**

`[TC — mais devagar]`

And this is the strangest part. I'm exhausted. And I still fight to stay awake. I keep doing things, I keep scrolling, like I'm actively resisting sleep.

`[TEXT: TIRED BUT WIRED]`

People have a name for this feeling: *tired but wired*. Your body is done, but your mind is still running, like the day hasn't let go of it yet. From what I've read, stress can keep the body in alert mode, even at night, when it should be slowing down. `[checar fonte]` And maybe that's why the phone feels so good at that hour. It's not rest. It just keeps my brain busy enough that I don't have to feel how tired I am.

### 5 · O custo real

`[TC — mais devagar]`

And then I wake up already tired. Not "I need a coffee" tired. Tired the whole day. I don't perform the way I want to at work. I feel sleepy in the afternoon. And every time I go to bed late, there's the same feeling: regret, and the certainty that tomorrow is already a little bit ruined.

Most adults need at least seven hours of sleep. `[checar fonte]` On a lot of nights, I'm not even close.

There's another cost I don't talk about much. I'm trying to lose some weight, and I know bad sleep makes that harder. There's research linking short sleep to the hormones that control hunger. `[checar fonte]` So the hours I steal at night, I pay back during the day, in more ways than one.

### 6 · A virada: a semana em que eu dormi bem

`[BR: fotos/vídeos das férias — o parque, árvores, a família]`

`[TC — softer]`

The last time I really slept well was on vacation. We stayed near a park, close to nature. I was with my family, so my phone was mostly forgotten in a corner. And my mom and my sister took care of the cooking.

And I slept. Easily. Like I hadn't in years.

`[TC — direct]`

At first I thought it was the nature. The fresh air. And that probably helped. But when I think about it now, I think the biggest difference was something else. For a few days, somebody took the three hundred little tasks off my plate. Nobody needed me to do the dishes or prepare the lunches. The day ended when it ended.

`[TEXT: it was never about the night]`

So maybe my problem was never the night. It was the day. If the only free time I get is the time I steal from sleep, then sleep is always going to lose.

### 7 · O que estou tentando

`[TC]`

I'm not going to give you a perfect night routine. I don't have one.

`[BR: velas acesas, luz baixa, banheiro]`

What I have is a rule that actually worked for a while: no screens and no bright lights after nine. I light some candles, take a shower, and get ready for bed slowly. When I did that, it really helped.

The hard part is keeping it. Because the rule takes care of the night, but it doesn't do anything about the day. The dishes are still there.

`[HANI — decidir se fala do CBD (ver notas de produção). Se sim, uma frase neutra:]`
*Some nights I also use CBD — but that's my own choice, not advice. Please talk to a doctor before trying anything like that.*

### 8 · Fecho — um cuidado possível + despedida íntima

`[TC — warm]`

So this week, I'm not trying to be perfect. I'm trying two things.

The candle rule, but only on weeknights. The weekend can be the weekend.

And one small piece of free time that I take during the day instead of stealing it at night. Even twenty minutes. Because I shouldn't have to wait until two in the morning to feel like my time is mine.

`[TEXT: you shouldn't have to steal time from your own sleep]`

Tell me in the comments: what time did you actually fall asleep last night? And what's on your second shift?

`[TC — intimate sign-off]`

And if you're watching this at one in the morning... I see you. Maybe close this one, and finish it tomorrow. I'll still be here.

## Notas de produção
- **Abrir com a cena real gravada à noite** (sábado, TV, relógio). É o gancho de
  reconhecimento na versão Hani: ela mostra em vez de perguntar.
- **A virada das férias (bloco 6) é o coração.** Se tiver fotos ou vídeos da viagem,
  usar. Pedir autorização à mãe e à irmã se aparecerem.
- **CBD — decisão da Hani:** mencionar deixa o relato mais honesto, mas a legalidade
  varia por país e o YouTube pode limitar anúncios em vídeos sobre cannabis. Se
  mencionar, uma frase neutra, sem recomendar marca nem dose. Cortar não perde nada.
- **Sem conselho médico.** Se a dificuldade for insônia (deitar e não conseguir dormir),
  não só procrastinação, dizer isso e indicar procurar um profissional.
- **Comentário fixado:** "What time did you actually fall asleep last night?"
- **Inglês:** frases curtas de propósito pra gravar com fluidez. Treina antes as partes
  com present perfect continuous e condicionais ("I've been doing this for as long as
  I can remember", "If the only free time I get...").
