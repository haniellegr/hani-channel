## Entrevista (resumo — a fonte do roteiro)

- Ganhou cerca de 12 kg no último ano. Hoje usa tirzepatida, com acompanhamento de
  endocrinologista.
- Férias em maio/2026: dormia por volta das 23h, quase sem celular durante o dia e à
  noite; a irmã resolvia o que faltava em casa. No mesmo mês voltou à academia, de
  leve, depois de ~1 ano parada. A bioimpedância mostrou menos gordura e mais massa.
- Depois das férias até meados de setembro: +6 kg, comendo razoavelmente bem e
  treinando. É rigorosa com a alimentação.
- O peso da rotina: casa, trabalho, compras, cozinha, tudo sozinha.
- Sono hoje: acorda depois de 6–7h apertada pra ir ao banheiro e, em dia de trabalho,
  nem sempre consegue voltar a dormir e completar 8h. Dormir até tarde no fim de
  semana "não é a mesma coisa".
- Sentiu confusão e frustração. O endocrinologista se espantou: nem sendo totalmente
  sedentária ela deveria ter ganhado tanto.

## Descrição (rascunho)
In May, my body finally started responding. Four months later, I'd gained six kilos,
while eating well and training. This isn't a diet video. It's about what the weight of
an ordinary routine can do to a body, and why "just try harder" was never the answer.
Not medical advice: talk to your doctor. (fontes no fim)

### 1 · Gancho (0–30s)

`[BR: close na folha de maio, depois na de setembro]`

These are two body composition scans. Same person. Four months apart.

`[TC — close, calm]`

In the first one, I had just come back from vacation. I'd lost fat and gained muscle, and I'd barely started training again. In the second one, I'd gained six kilos. And during those four months, I was eating well, and I was training.

So what happened? That's what this video is about.

### 2 · O primeiro scan: maio

`[BR: fotos das férias — parque, família]`

`[TC]`

In May, I went back to the gym after almost a year without training. I started really light, just getting my body moving again.

`[HANI: os números de maio. Ex.: "My body fat went from X to Y, and my muscle mass went up by Z."]`

For something that light, that was a surprise. And when I look back, May was a very particular month. I was on vacation. I was sleeping around eleven. My phone was mostly forgotten during the day. And my sister was taking care of whatever the house needed.

### 3 · O segundo scan: setembro

`[BR: mercado, cozinha, potes de marmita, mochila da academia]`

`[TC]`

Then vacation ended, and my normal life came back. Work. The house. Groceries. Cooking for the week. All of it on me again.

`[HANI: os números de setembro.]`

Six kilos. In about four months. And I'm strict with food. I know I eat well. I kept going to the gym.

`[TC — slower]`

I honestly didn't understand it. I was frustrated. And when I showed my endocrinologist, even he was surprised. He told me that even if I'd been completely sedentary, I shouldn't have gained that much.

### 4 · Nomear e desnormalizar

`[TC]`

When this happens, the usual answer is: you're eating more than you think. You're not trying hard enough. It's discipline.

But is it, though? Because I didn't change how I ate. I didn't stop training. What changed was everything around it.

`[TEXT: ALLOSTATIC LOAD]`

There's a name in science for this kind of thing: *allostatic load*. It's the wear and tear your body takes from stress that never really switches off. Not one big stressful event. The small, constant ones. Every day. `[checar fonte]`

### 5 · O que mudou de verdade

`[TC]`

So I started comparing May and September. Not the food. The life.

**The load.** On vacation, I bought something for lunch here and there, and that was it. At home, I'm the one running everything. The house, the job, the shopping, the cooking. That doesn't stop when I sit down.

**The sleep.** On vacation, I fell asleep around eleven and slept through. Now, I usually wake up after six or seven hours needing to go to the bathroom. On a workday, there's often no time to fall back asleep and get my eight hours. And sleeping in on the weekend? I can do it. But it's not the same.

`[TC — careful]`

There's research linking short sleep to more hunger and to weight gain, and research linking chronic stress to how the body stores fat. `[checar fonte]` I'm not saying that explains everything in my case. My doctor and I are still figuring it out. But it changed the question I was asking myself.

### 6 · A virada

`[TC — direct]`

I spent months asking, "What am I doing wrong?" And I think the better question was, "What is my life doing to me?"

`[TEXT: it wasn't a willpower problem]`

Because the version of me that responded so well in May wasn't more disciplined. She was more rested. She had help. She wasn't on her phone all day. Same body. Same person. A lighter life.

### 7 · O que estou fazendo agora

`[TC]`

So here's where I am. I'm following up with my endocrinologist. I'm now on a medication he prescribed, tirzepatide, and I'll talk about that in its own video, because it deserves more than one sentence.

But I'm also trying to treat sleep and stress as part of my health, not something I deal with after everything else. That's what my last video was about, why I can't go to bed, and honestly, this one is the other half of the same story.

### 8 · Fecho

`[TC — warm]`

If you're doing everything "right" and your body isn't responding, I want you to hear this: it might not be about willpower. Look at your sleep. Look at how much you're carrying. And talk to a doctor, a good one, who looks at the whole picture.

`[TC — intimate sign-off]`

Your body isn't betraying you. It might just be tired. I'll see you next time.
